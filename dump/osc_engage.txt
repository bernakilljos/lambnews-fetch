# Budget Engagement Report 2026/27 

# Introduction 

From 22 September - 19 October 2025 , we engaged with people who live, work and 

study in Lambeth to better understand their priorities and perspectives around our 

budget challenge. 

The aim of this engagement exercise was to:  

> 

raise awareness amongst residents and stakeholders of the extent of 

Lambeth's budget challenge  

> 

ask for suggestions as to how to prioritise council spending, as well as asking 

for suggestions as to how to raise and save money 

During the engagement period we:  

> 

Hosted a survey on the council's engagement platform, GoVocal  

> 

Distributed hard copies of the survey to our libraries  

> 

Deployed our market researchers to libraries, leisure centres and markets , in 

addition to the Health and Wellbeing Bus and a Housing 'Meet the Landlord 

event' in order to assist with the completion of surveys  

> 

Attended several online and in person engagement meetings and events, to 

promote the survey and to have focused discussions on topics relating dire ctly 

to the survey

Statistical testing has identified several differences between groups in the borough –

particularly according to gender, ethnicity, age, disability, housing tenure, economic 

activity, and how frequently one is in contact with the council. Generally, residents 

who are male and residents who own their homes were more likely to prioritise more 

‘universal ’ issues – such as violence and ASB, waste and street cleaning, park s, and 

road maintenance. Meanwhile, residents who are female, Black / Blac k British, 

disabled, and/or live in social rented accommodation, were more likely to select 

priorities like looking after vulnerable children and adults, schools, housing homeless 

families, and council housing services.

shoots, sponsorship and private venue hire  (57%).

residents who are men, LGB+, White British and/or owner -occupiers were more 

likely to agree with almost all measures, particularly when compared to their female, 

heterosexual, Black / Bl ack British , and social renter counterparts .

# Easy Read survey responses 

We received 34 Easy Read survey responses .

In these , 48.5% of respondents agreed we should keep helping people who need 

care and housing, even if it means stopping doing other things. Just 15.2% 

disagreed, with the rest either not being sure or not answering. 

The top three priorities for spend were slightly different from our main survey , but 

helping vulnerable children was a priority across both surveys :

1.  Helping older people who need care 

2.  Helping children who need care 

3.  Schools 

The top three methods to save and raise money align with the results from our main 

survey: 1.  Make money by doing things like hosting events, filming, or renting out 

spaces (55.9%) 

2.  Rent out council buildings to other groups (50%) 

3.  Work with community groups to run services more cheaply (32.4%) 

Some respondents raised concerns about council communication and accessibili ty, 

suggesting the council should listen more to residents and not exclude those who 

are not online. There were also calls for better procurement, contract management, 

and ensuring value for money. 

Organisational survey responses 

We received 37 responses f rom people who said they represented an organisation. 

73% of these respondents indicated that maintaining services for vulnerable people 

was important or very important to them. 

The top four priorities for spend were slightly different from those who responded as 

individuals but helping vulnerable children was a priority for both. It is perhaps not 

surprising that support for voluntary and community groups was seen as a key 

priority for this cohort as most organisatio nal respondents were responding on behalf 

of a voluntary or community group :

1.  Looking after vulnerable children (11) 

2.  Looking after vulnerable adults (10) 

3.  Council housing services (10) 

4.  Support for voluntary and community groups (10) 

The top three methods to save and raise money from these respondents differ 

slightly from those who responded as individuals, with agreement around renting out 

council buildings , partnering with community organisations and expanding 

commercial partnerships . Recruiting volunteers also featured as a priority .

1.  Partner with community organisations to deliver services where than 

can help deliver them at a lower cost (24) 

2.  Rent out council buildings to other organisations (23 )

3.  Recruit volunteers to help run services (14 )

4.  Expand commercial partnerships (14) 

# Next Steps 

This engagement exercise has provided the counc il with significant insights into 

resident's views about budget priorities which have been considered by the council ’s

Corporate Leadership Team as part of developing recommendations for the council ’s

Medium Term Financial Strategy and 2026/27 Budget setting process. 

Over two -thirds of residents indicated that maintaining servi ces for vulnerable people 

was important or very important to them and that has been a core part of the council ’s budget setting approach. Many services for vulnerable people are statutory 

requirements for the council to carry out, and while ensuring every service is 

delivered in the most effi cient way possible is a key of aim of our financial 

transformation, protecting vulnerable people has been at the heart of the budget 

decisions the council has made. This is particularly true in relation to looking after 

vulnerable children which was the one of the top three priorities selected by 

residents in the sur vey to spend money on. The council has increased its spending 

on children and young people by over £32 million in the last three years, and our 

budget propos als are focused on investing in intervening earlier to help reduce the 

number of children taken into care, to boost the number of in -house foster carers and 

to support more children out of care and into more suitable and permanent 

accommodation and support that better suits their needs. This is a key aim of recent 

government policy changes to embed a Families First approach which the council 

welcomes alongside the additional government investment for support for children 

and young people made th rough the recent Fair Funding reforms. 

The most selected priority by residents was around cutting violence and anti -social 

behaviour. The council has successfully lobbied to increase community policing in 

recent years and has boosted its on street presenc e in town centres with street 

warden patrols alongside using CCTV to provide community reassurance and deter 

anti -social behaviour. We will continue to prioritise t ackling anti -social behaviour 

alongside the council ’s award -winning work to tackle violence against those most 

likely to experience it - young people and women and girls . The third most selected 

priority was waste, recycling and street cleaning. The council is prioritising this area 

in this budget and we will continue to improve and enhance stree t cleanliness and 

cracking down on fly tipping through enforcement and quick responses to reported 

incidents. 

Residents who completed the council ’s budget survey showed significant support for 

a range of ways to save or raise money that the council is alr eady progressing as 

part of our drive to use council taxpayers' money as efficiently as possible. That 

includes renting out council buildings to other organisations , with the council already 

having reduced its office footprint significantly in the last few years by reducing core 

council buildings from 14 down to 2 and progressing plans to rent out floor space in 

the Civic Centre subject to ag reeing appropriate lettin gs. The majority of respondents 

also supported partnering with community organisations to deliver services where 

that can help deliver them at a lower cost , which is a core part of the council ’s

service delivery model across a range of services including h ealth and social care 

and youth services. Res idents were also keen to see an expansion of commercial 

partnerships, including holding more commercial events to help generate income to 

maintain key services which is a core part of the council ’s Events Strate gy. There 

were also a range of organisational efficiency suggestions which are key features of 

the council ’s financial management approach, including reducing the number of senior staff at the highest grades in the council by 20%, reducing the number of 

co uncil directorates from seven to five and using technology to make services more 

efficient and effective for residents. 

We ’d like to express our thanks to all residents and community organisations who 

took the time to share their views and suggestions abou t how we can work together 

to tackle the council ’s budget challenge.

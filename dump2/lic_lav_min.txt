Title: Agenda item - La Vuelta - (45 Tulse Hill, London SW2 2TJ) Variation

Markdown Content:
Presentation by the Licensing Officer

The Sub-Committee was informed that this was an application for a variation to the current premises licence submitted by Stars Square Limited for the premises La Vuelta, 45 Tulse Hill, London, SW2 2TJ. The applicant sought permission to vary the hours for the sale of alcohol and to add late night refreshment for the following hours.

Sunday to Thursday

12:00pm to 22:30pm

Friday and Saturday

12:00pm to 00:30am

Friday and Saturday

23:00pm to 00:00am

Sunday to Thursday

07:30am to 23:00pm

Friday and Saturday

07:30am to 01:00am

Ten representations were received in relation to the application. These were submitted by the Licensing Authority, Regulatory Services and the Commercial and Technical Environmental Health Team (TEHT), together with eight local residents. The representations related to the prevention of public nuisance, the prevention of crime and disorder, and public safety. Copies of the representations were provided to the applicant, and the applicant had been invited to attend the hearing.

Licensing Officers had attempted to contact the applicant by email on two occasions, namely on 15 January 2026 and 23 January 2026. In attendance on behalf of the responsible authorities was Ms Marcia Green, together with Mr Matthew Onakoya representing the Commercial and Technical Environmental Health Team.

Two local residents were also in attendance, Miss Camilla Walker, whose representation appeared on pages 57 to 61 of the report, and Mr Peter Learnat, whose representation appeared on pages 71 to 73 of the report. The application form was contained on pages 45 to 52 of the report. Representations in opposition to the application were included on pages 53 to 80 of the report, and a copy of the existing licence was contained on pages 81 to 93 of the report.

Presentation by Interested Parties

Marcia Green, representing the Licensing Authority, said that:

*   The Licensing Authority submitted a representation due to concerns that the variation, as applied for, may undermined two licensing objectives, namely the prevention of public nuisance and the prevention of crime and disorder. The applicant sought to extend the hours for the sale of alcohol, add late night refreshment on Fridays and Saturdays, and extend the overall opening hours. These hours were significantly later than those permitted under the existing licence.
*   Under the Council’s current licensing policy, the premises was classed as a restaurant located within a local shopping parade. For this type of premises in this location, the recommended terminal hours were 23:00 hours from Sunday to Thursday and midnight on Fridays and Saturdays. The hours requested by the applicant exceeded these recommendations.
*   The premises was situated within a residential area, with homes located directly above, opposite, and within the surrounding streets. This factor was considered significant when assessing the potential for late-night disturbance. Extending alcohol sales until 12:30 am and opening hours until 1:00 am increased the likelihood of late-night noise and disturbance affecting local residents, particularly those residing above the premises.
*   The Licensing Authority also raised concerns regarding the potential for disorder associated with later operating hours. At such times, customers were more likely to have consumed alcohol elsewhere prior to attending the premises. The risk was considered high that individuals arriving at, or passing by, the premises may have been intoxicated.
*   No additional conditions were proposed by the applicant to mitigate these risks, and no new conditions or controls were offered within the operating schedule. On this basis, the Licensing Authority recommended that the Committee refused the application.

Mathew Onakoya, representing the Technical Environmental Health Team (TEHT), said that:

*   Mr Onakoya stated that the Commercial and Technical Environmental Health Team (TEHT) had recorded 29 complaints since 2023 relating to excessive sound breakout and antisocial behaviour associated with the premises. During 2024, the department received a further eight complaints, and in 2025, five additional complaints were recorded.
*   Local residents were disturbed to learn that the premises sought to extend its operating hours, particularly following the submission of numerous complaints to the Council and to TEHT. Mr Onakoya further noted that on two separate occasions disturbances had escalated to the point of police attendance.
*   Mr Onakoya expressed the view that, should the extended hours be granted, serious issues would arise for local residents, and the applicant would continue to disregard the licensing objectives.

Ms. Camilla Walker, representing the Local Residents, said that:

*   Ms. Walker wished to note her representation and did not wish to add anything further.

Peter Learnat, representing the Local Residents, said that:

*   Mr Learnat stated that the premises was characterised under the Lambeth Licensing Policy as a restaurant within a local centre shopping parade; however, the premises was not situated within a shopping parade but was positioned at the end of a corner site facing a block of flats.
*   The Regulatory Services and Technical Environmental Health Team had accurately described the location within their representations. The premises was located beneath adjoining residential accommodation, which meant that, under the Lambeth Licensing Policy, the latest permitted opening time was 11:00 pm, as referenced within the agenda on pages 68 and 69.
*   Mr Learnat also noted that the relationship between the applicant and local residents had become increasingly strained. In early May 2023, a street party took place alongside the restaurant without any prior notice or warning, attracting crowds of people. The event included a bouncy castle, food sales, and a mobile loudspeaker that played music at such a volume that residents were forced to evacuate their flats for the duration of the party. Local residents raised concerns with their local councillor, who advised that the premises had received permission for the event.
*   Residents subsequently submitted a Freedom of Information (FOI) request regarding the restaurant and the street party. The Council responded that street party permissions had been granted for a residential event, with an application recorded for 7 and 8 May 2025.
*   However, the Licensing Team held no record of receiving such a request. In summary, the restaurant applied for a residential street party, which was refused because they acted as a co-organiser, and they subsequently failed to submit an application to permit a commercial event. The behaviour of this licensee, therefore, demonstrated clear disregard for local residents and their concerns.

Adjournment and Decision

At 20:11pm, the Sub-Committee withdrew from the meeting together with the legal advisor and clerk to deliberate in private.

The Sub-Committee had heard and considered representations from Marcia Green, representing the Licensing Authority, Mathew Onakoya, representing Technical Environmental Health Team (TEHT) and Ms Camilla Walker and Mr Peter Learnat, representing Local Residents.

Legal advice was given to the Sub-Committee on the options open to them and the need for any decision to be proportionate. The Sub-Committee decided to refuse the variation application for the following reasons:

*   The Licencing Subcommittee considered Regulation 22, the Licencing Act 2003, Hearings, Regulations 2005 in this regard. The Licencing Subcommittee, otherwise known as the LSC, did not consider that the licencing objectives were met. This was particularly with reference to the application for extended Opening hours, the LSC considered the policy at paragraph 16.5 at page 31 and Appendix 5 at page 51.
*   The preferred hours for a restaurant within local centre shopping parade was Sunday to Thursday to 20:00 hours Saturday and Friday and Saturday till midnight. No specific start time was recommended. Regarding the application for late night refreshments, the Licencing Sub-Committee considered the policy at paragraph 16.10 at page 32.
*   The Licencing Sub-Committee would also be considered more flexibly within the safe local conditions and circumstances to reflect the potentially lower impact of these types of premises. Notwithstanding paragraph 2.1 of the statutory guidance, revised guidance under Section 182 of the Licencing Act, stated that the Licencing Authority should look to the police as the main source on advice on crime and disorder.
*   However, the Licencing Subcommittee did not consider the licencing objectives were met, and the regarding the application for late night refreshment, notwithstanding that this will be considered more flexibly, the licencing objectives were not considered to be met in this regard, and the Licencing Sub-Committee has refused the application.

RESOLVED: To refuse the variation application.

Announcement of Decision

Members returned to the meeting and the Chair informed those present of the decision to refuse the variation application and provided reasons for the decision as outlined above. The Sub-Committee had considered all the options available to them and ultimately felt that the application and conditions met the concerns which had been raised. The Chair confirmed that written notification of the decision would be sent in due course.

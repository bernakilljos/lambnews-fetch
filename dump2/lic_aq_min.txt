Minutes:

Presentation by the Licensing Officer

The Sub-Committee was informed that this was an application for a review of the current premises licence brought forward by the Metropolitan Police, following a number of incidents that had occurred at the premises. In attendance for the Metropolitan Police was PC David Watson and on behalf of the applicant was Mr David Dadd’s. One representation was submitted by the Licensing Authority supporting the polices review of the premises.

The application form from the police could be found on pages 104 to 105 of the report. The Licensing Authority representation could be found on pages 107 to 108 of the report. The petition support of the premises was on pages 109 to 110 of the report, and a copy of the premises licence was on pages 111 to 121.

Presentation by the Applicant

Mr. David Dadd`s, Legal representative for Aquarium Tapas Bar, informed the Sub-Committee that:

*   Mr Dadd firstly thanked the Metropolitan Police Service and colleagues for meeting with him on several occasions during the review period. He also expressed his thanks to Democratic Services and the Licensing Officers for their work throughout this time.
*   He stated that the review had centred on a single isolated incident in March 2025, which had not repeated itself. He acknowledged that the licensing objectives had been undermined; however, those matters had been addressed. He confirmed that they had been addressed through conditions, with the Police acting as the principal source of advice on crime and disorder, and that the Police had been satisfied that the proposed steps would promote the licensing objectives.
*   Mr Dadd`s noted that the licence had originally contained 15 conditions. He stated that the revised conditions submitted to the Committee and the Licensing Authority would, in effect, supersede the existing conditions in order to tidy up the licence. He confirmed that, following discussions between himself, the Police, and the Licensing Authority, condition number 8 had been removed, resulting in 14 conditions.
*   He further stated that it had been agreed that these conditions would replace the previous conditions in their entirety and that the exercise had been intended to rationalise the licence.

In response to questions from Members, the Mr Dadd`s confirmed:

*   Mr Dadd stated that, during several meetings with the Police and the licensee, it had been established that the Police lacked confidence in the existing DPS. He stated that both he and the Police had held concerns regarding the DPS and whether he had exercised sufficient control over patrons.
*   However, he confirmed that the licensee had since addressed those concerns through the appointment of a new DPS. He further stated that the proposed DPS held a personal licence, which had addressed all concerns going forward.
*   Mr Dadd also noted that matters arising at the premises had been very rare and stated that, with the change of DPS and adherence to closing times, the premises had benefited from significant support from local residents. He further stated that the premises had been considered unique and bespoke in nature, which had informed why the proposed conditions had been regarded as more appropriate.
*   He confirmed that the licensee had installed a Licensing Connector system, which had been designed by Mr Dadd, and that the system had been implemented at the premises. He further stated that training for all staff had been provided, together with the introduction of new incident logs, thereby establishing a clear system of working.

Presentation by Interested Parties

PC David Watson, Representing Metropolitan Police, informed the Sub-Committee that:

*   PC Watson stated that he had submitted the application in May 2025. He and Mr Dadd’s had reached an agreement, and he genuinely believed that the proposed measures were amenable and acceptable to the Police.
*   PC Watson further stated that he genuinely believed the agreed conditions, together with all documentation submitted, would have been agreeable to the Committee Members and Local Authority.
*   He confirmed that all evidence provided by the applicant had satisfied the Police’s concerns in relation to crime and disorder, particularly regarding how those matters would have been addressed. He further stated that there had been general agreement regarding the removal of the DPS and believed that all parties had been broadly aligned on that point.

Adjournment and Decision

At 20:38pm, the Sub-Committee withdrew from the meeting together with the legal advisor and clerk to deliberate in private.

The Sub-Committee had heard and considered representations from PC David Watson, representing the Metropolitan Police, Mr David Dadd`s, Legal representative of Aquarium Tapas Bar and Marcia Green, representing the Licensing Authority.

Legal advice was given to the Sub-Committee on the options open to them and the need for any decision to be proportionate. The Sub-Committee decided to agree the conditions for the following reasons:

*   The Licensing Sub-Committee heard a review application concerning the Aquarium Tapas Bar, located at 415 Wandsworth Road, London, SW8 2JP. The Licensing Sub-Committee heard representations from PC David Watson of the Metropolitan Police Service and from Mr David Dadd`s on behalf of the premises licence holder.
*   PC Watson advised the Licensing Sub-Committee that dialogue had taken place with the licence holder and that a revised set of 14 conditions had been agreed. He stated that those conditions had been considered sufficient to ensure that the licensing objectives would be met if imposed. Mr Dadd`s confirmed that discussions had taken place and that agreement had been reached regarding the conditions, subject to the removal of condition number 8.
*   The Licensing Sub-Committee noted that no further matters had arisen that had undermined the licensing objectives since March 2025 and that the premises had benefited from a level of support from local residents, together with a change in the DPS.
*   The Licensing Sub-Committee considered paragraph 2.1 of the Section 182 Statutory Guidance, which advised that Licensing Authorities should look to the Police as the principal source of advice on matters relating to crime and disorder. The Sub-Committee recognised that the prevention of crime and disorder had formed the primary licensing objective under consideration. Having considered the representations and the guidance, the Licensing Sub-Committee resolved to modify the premises licence by the addition of the agreed conditions

RESOLVED: To agree the conditions.

Announcement of Decision

Members returned to the meeting and the Chair informed those present of the decision to agree the conditions and provided reasons for the decision as outlined above. The Sub-Committee had considered all the options available to them and ultimately felt that the licensee/applicant/amendments/conditions met the concerns which had been raised. The Chair confirmed that written notification of the decision would be sent in due course.

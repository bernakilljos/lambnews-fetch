**Contact for enquiries:**Peter Hesketh: Deputy Director of Finance,phesketh@lambeth.gov.uk.

Minutes:

The Chair introduced the agenda item and noted that the Committee had looked at the first set of savings proposals at its meeting in November 2025 and the Council remained in a very challenging financial position. The Committee was asked to review an updated set of savings proposals at this meeting and consider whether the Council would be able to close the funding gap and would the results still allow for Lambeth’s services to deliver to the standard expected by residents.

The [Acting Cabinet Member for Finance](https://moderngov.lambeth.gov.uk/mgAi.aspx?ID=67071), Councillor Judith Cavanagh introduced the report and noted that:

*   The report contained an additional set of savings that were necessary in light of the financial pressures on the Council’s budget. It was noted that those financial pressures continued to be experienced in the Council’s larger service areas such as provisions for those in temporary accommodation, Adult Social Care and Children's Services.
*   While the Council was facing those financial pressures, it was still performing well and was delivering a good quality of service. This was demonstrated by the ‘Good’ rating that was achieved recently by the borough’s Youth Justice Service.
*   The Government’s commitment to supporting local authorities to deliver for their residents was demonstrated by providing them with multi-year financial settlements to help them plan more effectively for the future. The Council was pleased that it had received a larger financial settlement from the Government than had been predicted, but it was still not sufficient to bridge the funding gap, therefore, they would be lobbying the Government for further additional financial support. The Council had been granted a transition arrangement of three years to help it improve its financial position, but this may be inhibited by the fact it was one of four local authorities that had been ‘capped’ by the Government, therefore the four local authorities were in discussions with Ministers regarding this matter.
*   It was refreshing that there was finally a Government in place that was committed to improve the lives for the country’s citizens and their commitment to lift the two-child limit which restricted the additional support for children on Universal Credit (UC) was an example of this. The Council estimated approximately 4,000 children would benefit from this policy to help to lift children out of poverty.
*   In conclusion, the Acting Cabinet Member for Finance was confident they would be able to close the funding gap with measures outlined which also allowed them to propose a balanced budget for 2026-27 leading to a much more secure financial position in 2027-28.

The Chair invited witnesses to speak on the agenda item.

Simon Hannah, Assistant Branch Secretary, UNISON, informed the Committee that:

*   There was some good news as the pessimistic predictions on funding from Government had not materialised, but local government funding was still less than it had been in 2010 under the last Labour Government.
*   The Council needed to be cautious of the over-reliance on Artificial Intelligence (AI) and he noted that recent research had revealed that between 70% and 85% of AI projects fail to meet their expected outcomes leading to the public sector consistently faced with even higher risks because its data was subject to much more strict controls.
*   Concerns related to employment of consultants was raised at the November 2025 meeting of the Committee and it was stated that the unions were told that challenge would be welcomed in this area, but in practice it did not seem the case when the unions had made representations in this area.
*   He expressed concerns that the unions had not received any feedback from the Council regarding the points raised at the meeting in November 2025 regarding information in the report stating that cuts to staffing would lead to significant detrimental impacts in 22 areas around Equity, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI). They noted that they had also reached out to the Committee and Council officers regarding this.
*   He was also concerned about consultations that the Council was engaging in, with surveys to residents on certain matters, because this could lead to the Council avoiding scrutiny via representatives of the workforce or residents through the unions. He suggested that other methods could be considered such as a borough conference which would help to prevent issues arising from unfavourable reports from the auditors, who were critical of the Council's failure to produce proper reports.

Susan Matthews, Lambeth Unite Trade Union Branch Secretary, informed the Committee that:

*   The issues regarding the EIAs and feedback to the unions had already been raised but did state that the report mentioned that the second tranche of savings proposals had now been reviewed by the Equity and Justice Panel and were recommended for approval by Cabinet to contribute to closing the funding gap. However, the unions had not received any information regarding this and was another example of the lack of transparency in communication between the Council and the union representatives.
*   The papers presented to the Committee discussed the risks regarding closing the funding gap but there was no analysis of how changes in terms of EDI were going to impact the workforce and the budget. The papers also denoted that there had been consultation including officers and members across the organisation – but the unions had not been consulted with as yet.
*   The report stated that there were no risks in terms of risk management and that was a concern as there would be an expectation that some analysis of what the risk may be if the Council’s savings plans and budget proposals did not go as planned.

Patricia Ennis, Lambeth GMB Branch Secretary, informed the Committee that:

*   The report states in relation to the Resources Directorate that as a whole it was currently forecasting an overspend position for the current year with the main pressures relating to staffing, which accounts for almost all of the controllable budget. Therefore, she stated that the introduction of the Spend Control Panel was intended to prevent such financial pressures and this was inflated by the fact that consultants were seemingly embedded in the operation of the Council. It was noted that the previous Committee meeting in November 2025, GMB representatives had requested a review of the Spend Control Panel.
*   She had concerns regarding the levels of reserves that the Council held and queried why the reserves seemed to be dwindling when the Council had instituted programme of savings from before 2024 and it was noted that the savings for Housing Services didn’t seem to be reflected in both the ‘New Savings Proposals’ and the ‘Total Savings by Directorate including November Proposals’ and this was not a true reflection of the position.

In response to questions from Members of the Committee, the Cabinet Member and officers explained the following:

*   The Chair noted that the Committee had requested information at its meeting in November 2025 regarding the Equality Impact Assessments (EIAs) that had been analysed as part of the savings process and the Committee had not received a response to the recommendation prior to this meeting. The Corporate Director of Resources advised the Committee that officers had developed initial impact assessments to assess the impact of the changes proposed but the full EIAs would be completed by Budget Council. She also noted that it was common practice to summarise those EIAs as part of the report that would be presented to Budget Council. She also added that the Trade Union representatives would also be formally consulted as part of the Joint Strategy Forum which was due to take place in February 2026.

*   Responding to Committee Members’ questions on their concerns about the plans for addressing the funding gap if the amount of money received by the Government was less than expected via Exceptional Financial Support (EFS), the Acting Cabinet Member for Finance advised the Committee that the Council was in negotiations with the Government regarding the EFS. She noted the issues related to budget were not new and therefore the case being made to the Government was based on plans to realistically close the funding gap. In responding to Committee Members on the frontloading of savings for 2026-27 and what had been outlined previously, the Corporate Director of Resources stated the savings were the same previous plans outlined in November 2025 but updated to the current position.

*   Committee Members queried whether an issue in closing the funding gap might lead to assets having to be sold off by the Council. It was also mentioned by Committee Members that in terms of temporary accommodation the report stated that, “in 2024-25 the £100m of expenditure was only matched with £40m of rental income resulting in a £60m burden on the Council”.The Corporate Director of Resources advised the Committee that the final figure had not been agreed with the Government and the Council would also look at a broader plan related to capital assets and identifying areas to reduce the need for borrowing. The Acting Cabinet Member for Finance added that in her opening remarks to the Committee, she had stated that the Council recognised that its big service areas such as temporary accommodation were being tackled to help improve its financial position.

*   The Chair stated that the Council should look to re-model services and invest to save, while doing so, any changes enacted should not have a negative impact on users of Children’s Services and Adult Social Care. The Corporate Director for Integrated Health and Care advised that the work being conducted in Adult Social Care was building on developments that were already taking place in the Directorate which was focused on supporting service users to be as independent as possible without any detriment to the package offered. He also added that the overall scale of savings for Adult Social Care for 2026-27 was about 1.4% of the budget so very achievable. He also noted as the service users were supported to be more independent and/or treated more people at home, care providers would be able to review packages on a regular basis to guarantee that the services were still suitable. The Committee noted the good work that had been scrutinised by the Children's Services Scrutiny Sub-Committee to address cost pressures and it was also noted that the Council needed to continue the theme of investing to save which helped to reduce costs while remodelling services. The Committee also noted that this was demonstrated by the policies the Council was implemented to address the cost pressures related to temporary accommodation. In responding to Committee Members’ questions regarding the impact of the saving proposal which stated that the Council would be supporting more children to leave care before the age of 18, the Director of Integrated Children's Commissioning and Youth advised the Committee that the savings proposal would affect approximately 30 children.
*   Responding to Committee Members’ questions about concerns regarding the risk management of the Council’s reserves due to the lack of detail in the reports regarding the challenges may inhibit the Council’s objective to establish general reserves at a level of 10% of net revenue expenditure, the Corporate Director of Resources advised the Committee that the Council intended to manage the risk regarding the Council’s reserves by holding reserves and balances at an appropriate and sustainable level. This was set out in the financial papers provided to Cabinet in July 2025 that stated the Council’s objective was to build up the general reserves (i.e. unearmarked balances) to 10% of net revenue expenditure. Additionally, she noted that the Council’s Reserves Strategy would set out the Council’s approach to ensuring that the level, purpose and planned use of its reserves were appropriate in the current financial climate. This would guide the Council to the position of building reserves of 10% of net revenue expenditure by the end of the third year of the Medium-Term Financial Strategy.

*   Committee Members asked about the assets and the outstanding accounts for Homes for Lambeth (HfL) and the impact this may have on the Council’s plans to stabilise its financial position. The Corporate Director of Resources advised that the Council’s Accounts for 2024/25 had been published as part of the agenda for the Corporate Committee meeting on 4 February 2026. She also noted that assets for HfL would not be ‘written off’ and would be declared as ‘impaired assets’ which would denote that it was possible that the Council may not receive the income owed. The Council would look to close down the remaining HfL companies with as little distress as possible to the Council’s financial position. The Council would also look to use some of the remaining housing for temporary accommodation where possible.
*   Responding to Committee Members’ questions on consultants following on from comments made by union representatives, both the Acting Cabinet Member for Finance and the Director of Integrated Children's Commissioning and Youth advised the Committee that there were occasions where it was appropriate to employ consultants on particular projects and noted the example of supporting Children’s Services to identify savings in terms of independent fostering agencies.
*   Committee Members asked for details regarding the saving related to the Lambeth Country Show, which was stated as £1m in the report by ‘no longer host(ing) the Lambeth Country Show’. The Corporate Director of Resources advised the Committee that if the Council did not host the Country Show, it would generate more income via the operators from the events that were held at Brockwell Park because the operators would not be able to contain the costs which would impinge on the possible income from the events. Therefore the £1m included the direct cost to the Council and the operator of the events that take place in Brockwell Park.
*   Responding to Committee Members’ questions related to the proposed savings which involved a ‘review of parking charges’, the Director of Climate Change, Planning and Transport advised the Committee that officers could update the Committee on the plans outside of the meeting regarding the charging adjustments that would be required to meet the savings. He also noted that the Council was now operating a new Parking Strategy alongside its Kerbside Strategy while moving towards a more Emissions-Based Parking Policy. Committee Members asked for more detail about the Council’s plans to understand the impact on residents if parking charges were increased.

*   Committee Members asked the proposed savings which involved the ‘Bandstand in Clapham Common Rent Increase’ savings proposal. Additionally, Committee Members requested more detail on the Council’s plans for the Bandstand in Clapham Common if a rent increase was confirmed.The Director of Regeneration, Growth & Capital Investment advised that he would write to the Committee outside the meeting and the Director of Climate Change, Planning and Transport confirmed that this was part of a lease renewal process.

*   The Chair raised queries about the Council’s plans for addressing the level of legal housing disrepair cases and was concerned that the plans were not robust enough.The Corporate Director of Resources added that the Council had devised an improvement plan of action to reduce the level of housing disrepair cases. The Chair noted that this was a major issue, and the level of cases had been problematic for a long time in Lambeth. The Corporate Director of Resources advised the Committee that the Finance, Legal and Housing Services were working together to address the problem, and this included commissioning external lawyers to support the Council’s work to resolve the cases quickly and fairly.
*   Committee Members noted that it was sometimes difficult to understand the impact of the suggested savings because some of the headings in the report were quite general and if more detail was provided with clearer descriptions this would support Members’ understanding of the proposals when the report was presented at Budget Council.

*   Committee Members noted that the information in the reports provided was sometimes difficult to follow with a statement of ‘£722k total savings across three areas (Increase in-house foster carers; Prevention of care starts; and Routes out of care’ did not correspond to a figure of £750k at a different part of the report. The Corporate Director of Resources advised that officers were still working on the final report that would be presented at Budget Council so could incorporate the changes suggested and she would review the figures related to total savings mentioned by Committee Members.

RECOMMENDATIONS:

1.   The Council to ensure that the Committee receives the findings of the Equalities Impact Assessments and these are taken onboard when determining savings plans. These should also be made public in some format so they could be viewed by residents.

1.   The Council to ensure that as far as possible that Lambeth invests to save and any changes to Adult Social Care and Children’s Services do not impact negatively to those receiving these services. If negative impacts result, the changes are reviewed with users to mitigate them.

1.   The Council to ensure that steps are taken to increase and safeguard the reserves and general reserves are built up to 10% of net revenue expenditure within three years as planned.

1.   The Council to continue to keep the Committee updated on a quarterly basis on the tracking and delivery of the proposed savings, especially in relation to temporary accommodation, social care, interest costs on borrowing and legal costs related to housing disrepair cases.
2.   The Council to keep the Committee updated on the Exceptional Financial Support (EFS) application and provide the Committee with the steps that would be taken to meet the budget gap if the EFS settlement is not provided as anticipated in 2026-27.
3.   [The Council to ensure that](https://moderngov.lambeth.gov.uk/mgAi.aspx?ID=67071)housing disrepair cases are properly monitored and performance improved, particularly in relation to damp and mould. Additionally, the Council to ensure that the Housing Scrutiny Sub-Committee is kept updated on monitoring outcomes and legal housing disrepair cases.

1.   The Council to ensure that the savings and spending proposals that were presented to Budget Council provided more detail to provide more clarity in their description.

1.   [The Council to write to the Committee to outline the plans for the parks restructure and the proposed increases in parking charges to understand their impact on residents. Additionally, the Council to write to the Committee regarding the plans for the Clapham Common Bandstand.](https://moderngov.lambeth.gov.uk/mgAi.aspx?ID=67071)

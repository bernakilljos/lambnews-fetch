Minutes:

The Chair introduced the item and stated that developer contributions helped the Council to deliver the infrastructure and services communities needed to support the developments being built in their communities. This meeting would give the Committee an opportunity to assess how well the Council was performing in terms of distributing those developer contributions.

Deputy Leader (Housing, Investment and New Homes), Councillor Danny Adilypour, introduced the report and noted that:

*   The report provided a comprehensive overview of the Council’s approach to distributing developer contributions and it also provided some examples of how the Council was ensuring that the money was spent to benefit communities across the borough.
*   He noted that there were three main ways in how the Council obtained developer contributions - Community Infrastructure Levy (CIL), Neighbourhood CIL (NCIL) and Section 106 planning obligations (S106). The Committee heard how the report outlined what the Council was doing to improve and highlight transparency, improve delivery of the projects and what it was also doing to ensure that the distribution of developer contributions aligned with the Council’s Growth Plan, Lambeth Local Plan, Infrastructure Delivery Plan and Borough Plan.
*   The report also provided an overview of development contributions received by the Council as of December 2025, along with projections of what this is likely to look like in the years ahead, although with the caveat that this was subject to change due to broader economic conditions, variations in development activity, and potential changes in national policy or legislation.
*   Officers provided additional information as requested by Committee Members and he welcomed any comments or recommendations which helped the Council to build on the work it was already delivering in this policy area.

The Chair invited witnesses to speak on the agenda item.

Tayo Adesina, Trash Gxng, informed the Committee that:

*   He was a director for Trash Gxng which was a grassroots social mobility organisation, ultimately equipping young people from the ages 11-25 with the creative and employability skills they required. Trash Gxng focused on supporting those who are marginalised in the community and often those who may be economically disadvantaged or facing multiple barriers, with Special Educational and Disabilities (SEND) and mental health needs as examples.
*   Trash Gxng received Neighbourhood Community Infrastructure Levy (NCIL) funding and they were able to demonstrate their impact on the young people it supported via the fact that 99% participants were strongly enjoying and engaged in the sessions provided and 90%+ of young people reported that they had increased self-belief and empowerment to make better decisions for themselves. He also noted that Trash Gxng were also able to launch programmes through the support of this funding and the aid of the Council’s ‘Lambeth Made’ programme.
*   It would be beneficial for organisations like Trash Gxng if their funding was more stabilised because if it lost its NCIL backing there was no guarantee that the Council were able to replace that funding. He added that it would be disappointing to stop operating when it could evidence over many years the positive impact their programmes and sessions had on young people’s lives.

Ted Inman, South Bank and Waterloo Neighbours (SoWN), informed the Committee that:

*   He was a representative from SoWN, which was a community organisation with very broad representation amongst local residents, workers, businesses, and voluntary groups in the area. He stated that SoWN worked closely and had a good relationship with the Council.
*   He noted that SoWN provided a statement in advance to Committee Members and while there was positive engagement with the Council over matters such as Section 106 funding it also stated that SoWN felt that there could be more transparency and engagement with community groups as part of the process of distributing developer contributions.
*   The approach in terms of Section 106 funding was specific to how it would be spent to benefit that immediate community where the development was due to be built, but as it currently stood, that immediate community which would bear the pressure of increased traffic and the available of green areas had no say in what mitigation measures were agreed. He also highlighted another example where Section 106 agreement for the Elizabeth House placed a value of £32.5m on 10% affordable workspace at a 50% discount over 15 years, a huge proportion of the total Section 106 package of some £40m. In comparison, the allocation for the nearby green space, Jubilee Gardens, was only awarded £50,000, It was also noted that the South Bank and Waterloo were important strategic neighbourhoods in Lambeth due to its local economy, alongside being a cultural and tourist destination for visitors, and this made it important that there was a mechanism to input local knowledge into the process to inform those strategic decisions.
*   They welcomed Lambeth’s decision to allocate £1m of NCIL funding to be prioritised by SoWN on projects assessed to be of the highest local importance. This at present was a one-off and though SoWN understood the financial pressures on the Council, it still felt that the way forward in looking at the best way to spend income generated by developer contributions would benefit from a closer working relationship between the Council and the local community.

The Chair thanked Trash Gxng, South Bank and Waterloo Neighbours,Planning Advisory Service (PAS) and Friends of Larkhall Park for their written statements in advance.

In response to questions from Members of the Committee, the Cabinet Member and officers explained the following:

*   The Chair asked how the Council prevented developers from using complex viability assessments to reduce or remove obligations to provide affordable housing or infrastructure. The Director of Climate Change, Planning and Transport explained that the report outlined the processes that Council officers looked to ensure that the viability assessment was applied to maximise the amount of affordable housing provided by new developments and to secure that planning obligations by developments were acceptable for Section 106 agreements. The Council also based its approach on the Local Plan and London Plan – therefore on public land the starting point was 50% of affordable housing and 35% on private land. If developers were not able to provide the minimum amount of affordable housing, the Council would assess planning applications to see how much affordable housing was viable, with the option of a late-stage viability review to see if it could be increased as part of the development. With sales of housing of new development ongoing, this would give the Council an understanding of an uplift split 60/40 in favour of affordable housing for the development.
*   Responding to Committee Members’ questions on how the Council ensured that projects that have been approved were viable to start with so it could access the developer contributions, the Director of Climate Change, Planning and Transport advised the Committee that the allocation of developer contributions was governed by a number of strategy documents such as the Local Plan and the Infrastructure Delivery Plan to ensure the development aligned with them. It was also noted that there had been the recent capital programme review which would alter the profile of developments in the future, compared to the past when the Council could have used a mixture of options to help support projects like using borrowing to generate more funds. The Director of Regeneration, Growth and Capital Investment added that the capital programme was being revised and there was acceptance that many developments would be more reliant on Section 106 agreements.
*   The Chair asked why so much of the developer contributions were unspent and the Director of Climate Change, Planning and Transport explained that one of the reasons for this was that many schemes often required the Council to provide matching funding to activate developer contributions. In responding to Committee Members’ questions on whether the Council could allocate the funding with the anticipation of receiving the required funding in future years to get developments started, the Head of Infrastructure, Operations and Innovation advised against this approach as it would be committing to developments without currently being able to source the funding at that moment in time.
*   Responding to Committee Members’ questions on the governance of CIL, NCIL and Section 106 funding, the Director of Regeneration, Growth and Capital Investment advised the Committee that the governance of each scheme was outlined in the report. It was noted that CIL monies could be allocated borough-wide (Lambeth treats the whole borough as ‘local’), NCIL funding was spent in consultation with local communities and Section 106 planning obligations were negotiated legal agreements on a case-by-case basis tied to specific planning applications.
*   Committee Members asked whether developers could be penalised if they did not provide the amount of affordable housing or could be compelled to provide the affordable housing first to meet the target. The Director of Climate Change, Planning and Transport explained that the Council had an extensive pre-application process where officers had frank discussions with developers regarding the amount of affordable housing they could realistically deliver as part of the project. Some developers in attempting to deliver on their planning agreement were inhibited on occasion by the inability to find a viable Registered Provider to support the project. He added that the Council recognised that in the current economic conditions, factors were difficult in this area, but wanted to secure as much new housing as possible – including affordable housing - it could.
*   Responding to Committee Members’ questions on why NCIL funding was not always spent in the immediate community where monies were generated, both the Director of Climate Change, Planning and Transport and Director of Regeneration and Growth and Capital Investment advised the Committee that a decision had been taken by the Cabinet on the designation of a ‘local area’. Additionally, the Cabinet agreed in July 2021 to formally revert to its position around the additional allocation of 10% CIL to a local level and with NCIL being 15% of CIL receipts for areas without a Neighbourhood Plan and 25% for areas with a Neighbourhood Plan.
*   Responding to Committee Members’ questions on providing more transparency on how developer contributions were spent to inform the Committee, the Director of Regeneration, Growth and Capital Investment advised the Committee that the Council was obliged to publish an annual Infrastructure Funding Statements (IFS) to improve transparency and report on spend and the allocation of CIL, NCIL and Section 106 funding.

The Members of the Committee agreed to extend the meeting by up to 30 minutes at 8.50pm.

*   Responding to Committee Members’ questions on whether there should be more political guidance on how developer contributions were spent, the Deputy Leader (Housing, Investment and New Homes) advised the Committee that the Council approached the distribution of developer contributions slightly differently given the type – CIL, NCIL and Section 106 funding. The overarching framework guided the Council to distribute CIL and NCIL monies across the borough where appropriate, whereas funding provided via Section 106 agreements had to be spent to benefit the local community where the development was situated.
*   The Chair asked how the Council engaged with the local community and residents regarding developer contributions. The Director of Regeneration, Growth and Capital Investment advised the Committee that consultation with local communities focused on informing the Council’s strategies, policies and programmes like the Borough Plan and Growth Plan as examples. The Head of Infrastructure, Operations and Innovation noted that alongside consultation on the strategic documents, there was consultation with local residents regarding issues like the charging schedules for CIL. The Deputy Leader (Housing, Investment and New Homes) added that consultation also took place around planning applications for developments which included Section 106 agreements where residents often raised issues important to them such as parking and access to green areas.
*   Responding to Committee Members’ questions on improving transparency regarding unspent developer contributions and the dangers of the Council having to return some of the contribution if not allocated for projects, the Director of Regeneration, Growth and Capital Investment advised the Committee that he would be working closely with the Head of Infrastructure, Operations and Innovation to consider options regarding the unspent developer contributions that the Council held. He added that one of the issues that had been previously raised was that some of the developer contributions required the Council to provide matching funding. The Director of Climate Change, Planning and Transport also confirmed that no developer contributions had been returned to developers at this stage. Committee Members asked whether the Council were able to borrow on the back of the unspent developer contributions and the Director of Climate Change, Planning and Transport stated that the Council did not initiate any borrowing by using the developer contributions.
*   Committee Members asked what options were open to the Council if developers did not honour agreements in terms of developer contributions. The Head of Infrastructure, Operations and Innovation noted that there were a number of enforcement or penalty actions that were open to the Council including: issuing a Stop Notice on the development, placing a liability order on the development via a Magistrate Court allowing the Council to use enforcement agents to recover the owed contributions; or they could place some other charges on the land. All options would be considered on a case-by-case basis.
*   The Chair asked how the Council looked to provide continual and sustainable funding for grassroot organisations who received funding from NCIL as an example. The Director of Regeneration, Growth and Capital Investment advised the Committee that in terms of the Community Connections Fund, the Council looked to provide grants for a longer-term rather than single yearly one-off awards to help with their sustainability, but would check on the example raised at the meeting. He also noted organisations involved in skills and training, the Council would generally utilise multi-year CIL/NCIL grants to support their objectives and the Council were reviewing its Section 106 funding for employment, training and skills for the best way to utilise them to support young people.
*   Responding to Committee Members’ on whether the Council would consider methods to demonstrate its allocation of developer contributions to residents, the Director of Climate Change, Planning and Transport advised the Committee that the Council was transparent in regard to its distribution of developer contributions by publishing its IFS because only approximately 17% of local authorities were publishing a statement regarding their current position. He also referred Committee Members to the statement from the Planning Advisory Service (PAS) which supported the Council approach in publishing an IFS but was willing to consider any best practice from other local authorities that may do it in a different way such as through a story map which linked developer contributions to planning applications and how that related to allocations/spending if feasible. The Head of Infrastructure, Operations and Innovation added that officers could consider reporting in a shorter period such as quarterly, if that would help with transparency.
*   Responding to Committee Members’ questions on whether the Council would consider changes to how it administered Section 106 funding to support areas that had more difficulties such as shared ownership, the Director of Climate Change, Planning and Transport advised the Committee that the Council would get the opportunity to review its local plan in the two-to-three years to update its policies in relation to affordable housing. He noted that shared ownership had been more difficult to access in parts of the borough but there were also other options that were available to residents including the intermediate rent option or non-self-contained housing, e.g. co-living, in the market. He concluded that a new draft for the London Plan was expected during Spring 2026 and this would begin the process for updating Lambeth’s Local Plan.

RECOMMENDATIONS:

1.   The Council to take steps to ensure that financial viability assessments do not provide loopholes for developers to escape their obligations and ensure that late stage viability reviews are carried out where feasible.

1.   The Council to ensure that every effort is made to allocate unspent Section 106 and Community Infrastructure Levy (CIL) funding to services that were in urgent need of investment.
2.   The Council to ensure that community groups and residents are encouraged to take part in discussions on the local plan so they are fully involved in deciding how any CIL is allocated and spent.
3.   The Council to ensure where possible, greater continuity of funding is provided for programmes especially for grassroots organisations that have demonstrated positive outcomes for disadvantaged and vulnerable groups.
4.   The Council to be more transparent and demonstrate the delivery funded by developer contributions which especially show what Section 106 funding has been received, the planning applications they relate to alongside what Section 106 funding had been spent in the area. The Council also looks for opportunities to strengthen the transparency of the governance, decision-making and the prioritisation processes.
